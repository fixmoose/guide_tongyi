1 How the system fits together

Two boxes get power and talk to each other. The outdoor heat pump is the brain; the indoor Hydro Box is the muscle (pump + the built-in DHW 3-way valve + expansion). The two 3 kW tank heaters are switched by contactors the brain controls.

① The heat pump (outdoor)

3-phase power + the control board. All sensors, the wired controller, smart-grid signals and the relay outputs that drive heaters / pump / valve land here.

② The Hydro Box (indoor)

Circulation pump, expansion vessel and the built-in DHW 3-way valve. Takes its own 1-phase supply + a few control wires from the heat pump.

2 Power supplies (4 circuits)

Heat pump + Hydro Box each get a dedicated circuit, and each 3 kW tank heater needs its own dedicated 1-phase circuit — the board only switches the contactor coil, not the heater current. Every circuit gets a 30 mA RCD.

❓ "How does a signal work on one wire?" — it doesn't

You're right: a signal always needs a return. On this board the return is either the next terminal in the pair, a shared common rail, or the shared neutral. So a function that looks like "one terminal" is really a loop. There are four kinds:

① Differential bus (A & B)

RS485: (6)+(7) = A1/B1, (8)+(9) = A2/B2. The two wires carry the +/− difference — this is your "A&B / +&−". Always a shielded twisted pair.

② Dry contact (signal + common)

Thermostat, Linked switch, SG, EVU, Solar: a volt-free switch closes the loop. Thermostat: L1 = common; close L1–H = heat call, L1–C = cool. Linked switch (10)–(11): close = enable. The board supplies the tiny sense voltage — you only open/close the loop.

③ NTC temperature sensor (2-wire)

Tank / buffer / floor sensors (TWout2, TACt1/2, TWout3): a thermistor whose resistance changes with temperature. 2 wires, polarity-free. They plug into dedicated sensor connectors, not the screw terminals.

④ Relay output (live + shared N)

A–E and 1–7: the terminal switches a live out; the return is the shared neutral E (zero line). So an output = its terminal + the common N. That's why there's one N for the whole output group.

Left: a dry-contact input is a loop between a signal terminal and its common (here L1). Right: a temperature sensor is a 2-wire thermistor on its own connector. Either way — never a single wire.

4 Point-to-point connections

Schematic-style, right-angle wiring for each device. The two 3 kW heaters read left → right: board → contactor → tank, with their own protected supply feeding the contactor and the thermal feedback returning to the board.

A · 500 L DHW tank heater (3 kW) — via contactor

How it works: the board puts 230 V on Out C → energises the contactor coil (A1/A2) → the main contact feeds the 3 kW element from its own protected supply. The element's thermal cut-out reports back to inputs 1 & 2. Neutral & earth go straight to the heater. Controller: Advanced → [Hot Water Function]=On, [Water Tank Heating]=On, [Water Tank Heating Power]=3000 W.

B · 200 L buffer tank heater (3 kW) — via contactor

Same pattern, different terminals: driven by Out B (aux/backup heating), feedback to inputs 3 & 4. Tops up the buffer on very cold days or as emergency heat. Controller: Advanced → [Electric Heater 1 Power]=3000 W (enable backup heating).
DHW 3-way diverter valve. Board Out 5 ↔ Hydro Box terminal 2. Small motor — wire direct, no contactor. It already lives inside the Hydro Box; you only land the control wire. Controller: [Hot Water Function]=On.
System circulation pump. Board Out A → Hydro Box terminal 3. Small modern circulator (<400 W) → direct. Add a contactor if the pump exceeds 400 W. Controller: [Pump Power] = actual max W.
Room thermostat — you usually don't need one. The wired controller is already a thermostat: mount it indoors and it senses room temperature for space heating (§7/§10). Only wire a separate stat to (1) L1 · (2) H · (3) C if a specific room needs its own additional thermostat. If you do fit one, the unit then controls water temperature only. If you fit none, leave (1)–(2) linked.

Aim for water velocity 0.6–1.2 m/s (quiet, low pressure-drop). The internal pump (Wilo Para 25/9) gives ≈ 9 m max head — plenty for a normal house, but long runs/many fittings eat into it (see heights & lengths).

💧 The pumps — there are two (Hydro Box is optional)

There's a pump built into the outdoor heat pump (Wilo Para 25/9) and — if you fit the Hydro Box — a second pump inside it. They do different jobs. This decides how you connect the floor or radiators.

Pump 1 — in the heat pump (primary)

The Wilo inside the outdoor unit circulates the primary loop (heat pump ↔ buffer). It runs whenever the heat pump runs and the unit needs constant free flow for heat transfer and defrost/freeze protection.

No flow is the heat pump's worst enemy. Starve it → low-flow fault, plate exchanger freezing, pump damage. There must always be an open path on this primary side.

Pump 2 — in the Hydro Box (secondary)

The pump inside the Hydro Box drives the distribution / secondary side — pushing water out to the buffer, DHW coil and the heating zones. Without the Hydro Box, the heat pump's own pump does it all (and you may need a field-supplied auxiliary pump for long runs).

Keep the primary side's open path with a buffer / low-loss header (best), an automatic bypass valve, or at least one always-open loop.

🏠 Underfloor manifold — not on the bare pump

Underfloor needs a lower water temperature (≈30–40 °C, via a mixing valve) and its own balanced flow — and a manifold whose actuators can close all loops would deadhead a pump feeding it directly.

Do this: feed the floor manifold from the buffer through a mixing station (mixing valve + its own small zone pump). Both heat-pump and Hydro Box pumps then stay on a free-flowing primary.

♨ Radiators — yes, if always open

A radiator circuit that's always open (lockshield-balanced, or at least one rad with no closing TRV, or an automatic bypass) can be driven by the Hydro Box pump directly — radiators run near the heat-pump flow temperature, so no mixing is needed.

If every radiator can close (smart TRVs everywhere), add an automatic bypass valve or a buffer so neither pump deadheads.

Short answer: the primary side (the Hydro Box pump's circuit) must stay free-flowing — no closing thermostats in its only path. Put the temperature regulation (mixing) and the room thermostats / zone valves on the secondary side, after a buffer, each with their own pump. For a single radiator circuit that never fully closes, the built-in pump alone is fine.

Pipe materials

✔ OK to use

  • Copper (correct fittings, deburr, no over-heat on brass).
  • PEX / PE-RT with an oxygen barrier (essential — no O₂ ingress).
  • Multilayer PEX-AL-PEX — ideal: rigid, O₂-tight.
  • PP-R (polypropylene), stainless steel.
  • Brass / DZR brass / bronze fittings & valves.

✘ Do NOT use

  • Galvanized / zinc-coated steel — corrodes against the copper inside the unit; precipitates with glycol.
  • Plain mild steel without treatment.
  • Non-barrier plastic pipe (lets oxygen in → sludge/corrosion).
  • Dissimilar bare metals touching (galvanic corrosion — isolate with dielectric unions).
  • Automotive glycol.

Heights, lengths & the outdoor run

How high / how far?

  • Building height is not limited by the pump — it's a sealed loop, so the static head cancels out. It's limited by your fill pressure (≈1.5 bar ≈ 15 m of height) and the 3-bar ceiling. Tall house → fill a little higher (stay < 3 bar) and size the expansion vessel for the water volume.
  • Pipe length is a head budget: pipe friction + filter + buffer + DHW coil + valves must stay under the ~9 m the pump gives at your flow. In 1″/DN25 this is generous for a normal house; if runs are long or fittings many, upsize to DN32 or add an auxiliary pump (the manual requires one if pressure-drop is too high).
  • Minimum water volume: the 200 L buffer easily covers the heat pump's need for stable running and defrost energy — no concern at 12 kW.

Outdoor pipe between unit & house

  • Keep it as short as practical; every meter outdoors is heat loss + freeze risk.
  • Insulate fully with closed-cell, UV-resistant, weatherproof lagging.
  • The outdoor water must be frost-protected — glycol (see below) and/or the unit's built-in antifreeze function with power kept on.
  • Fall the pipes so they can drain; protect against UV, rodents and mechanical damage.
  • In cooling, condensate can drip from the pipes — route/insulate so it can't damage anything.

💧 Condensate drain — where the water from the outdoor unit goes

In heating mode the outdoor coil collects moisture and frost; every defrost melts it and the water runs out of the drain hole in the very bottom of the outdoor unit (base pan). It can be several liters a day in cold, damp weather. This water has nothing to do with the heating circuit or the Hydro Box.

✅ Route it into soil — away from concrete

  • Let the water drain from the bottom hole into open soil or a gravel bed beside or under the unit, where it can soak away.
  • Keep it off concrete (slab, pedestal, terrace, walkway): water soaks into the concrete, freezes and cracks it, and makes an ice patch.
  • If the unit stands on a concrete pad, leave a gravel strip / gap under the drain hole or lead a short hose from the hole off the pad onto soil.
  • Mount the unit raised (pedestal / feet / wall bracket) so the hole is free and can't ice shut in snow.

🔧 Accessory bag & frost tips

  • The box includes 2 drain nipples, 2 PVC hoses and 3 rubber plugs — use them only if you need to lead the water away; plug any extra base-pan holes.
  • Any hose must slope down all the way and be short — a long hose freezes solid. Free dripping onto gravel/soil is the most frost-proof.
  • The unit's base-pan heater (DPH) keeps the tray ice-free — leave the unit powered in winter.
  • Hydro Box port ④ "drain hole" is only for emptying the water circuit, not for condensate.

🧥 Insulation

  • Insulate the entire circuit — flow and return.
  • Use closed-cell, vapor-tight lagging (e.g. EPDM/elastomeric) so it can't sweat in cooling mode.
  • ≥ 20 mm thickness where ambient > 30 °C and humidity > 80 % RH (anti-condensation).
  • Outdoor & DHW pipes: thicker + weatherproof; insulate valves/fittings too.

Which parts you actually need

🟢 With Hydro Box

The box already contains the pump, expansion vessel, 3-way valve, dirt filter, safety valve, gauge, auto air-vent and a ball valve on every port. You add, outside the box:

  • Insulated (glycol) pipes HP ↔ box, and box ↔ buffer / DHW / zones.
  • Magnetic filter on the return (recommended), drain taps, high-point air vents.
  • All DHW potable-side safety (T&P, PRV, expansion, non-return).
  • Tank sensors into pockets; zone shut-off valves.

🔵 Without Hydro Box

The heat pump has only water IN / OUT. You must field-supply the whole kit:

  • System circulation pump (+ auxiliary pump if needed).
  • Motorised ball 3-way valve (DN25, 230 V, < 90 s) for DHW diversion — only if there is also a heating circuit. A hot-water-only system needs no diverter valve.
  • Expansion vessel, 3-bar safety valve, gauge, fill/drain, auto air-vents.
  • Magnetic dirt filter + strainer on the return; isolating valves; flexible connectors.
  • Everything in the DHW potable list, plus all tank sensors.

6 How the 3-way valve & DHW priority work

One diverter at the heat-pump outlet (built into the Hydro Box) decides whether the heat goes to the DHW tank coil or to the buffer / heating.

Hot water only? Skip this whole section. The diverter exists to choose between hot water and space heating. With no heating circuit — heat pump straight into the DHW tank (Level 1 in §8) — there is nothing to choose, so no 3-way valve is required at all.
DHW below set point → valve driven to the DHW position; all heat to the tank. DHW has priority.
Tank satisfied → valve returns to heating; flow to the buffer, then your zones.
DHW priority sequence: if hot water and heating both call, the unit heats the DHW tank first, then flips the valve back to heating. Radiators/floor pause a few minutes during a DHW cycle — normal, not a fault.
The 3-way valve is already inside the Hydro Box — you only land the control wire (board Out 5 → terminal 2). If you do not use the Hydro Box, the installer must supply a 3-way valve: a motorised ball type, DN25, 230 V, < 90 s travel, for full separation between DHW and heating water.
Two valves, two jobs: the DHW diverter (Out 5 / term 2) chooses tank-vs-heating. A separate mixing valve (Out 6, field supplied) is only needed to blend down hot buffer water for low-temperature underfloor loops while radiators/FCU run hotter — see Zones (§7).

7 Zones explained

A "zone" is one group of emitters sharing a target. Up to two heating zones (A & B) + hot water. Each can be controlled by water temperature or by room temperature (the controller/stat as the room sensor).

Single zone (most common)

One emitter type — e.g. underfloor everywhere. Only Zone A. Simplest wiring; no mixing valve.

Two zones

Zone A hot (rad/FCU) + Zone B cooler (underfloor). Needs a mixing valve + pump on Zone B (Out 6/7, field supplied) to drop the water temperature.

Water-temp vs room-temp

Water temp: unit holds a flow temperature. Room temp: the wired controller measures the room and modulates to hold it. Set via [Room Temperature Control Scheme].

Controller "home screens" (pick the one that matches your house):
1 single zone · water temp + DHW
2 single zone · room temp + DHW
3 dual zone · water (A) + room (B) + DHW
4 dual zone · water (A) + water (B) + DHW
Underfloor caution: keep a floor zone's target temperature low — never set it to radiator temperatures, or you risk the screed/floor finish.

● Level 1 · Heat pump + DHW tank only (the simplest)

Just hot water — and no 3-way valve. With only a DHW tank on the system there is nothing to divert between, so the heat-pump flow and return go straight to the tank coil (the 2 pipes). No diverter valve, no buffer, no zone pump; valve output Out 5 and the buffer heater stay unused.

🎛 Controller setup sequence

  1. [Air Conditioning Type] — leave it as it comes. The factory default is [1] Heating & Cooling and it does not need changing for a hot-water-only install.
  2. [Hot Water Function] = On. This is what makes it a DHW system — not the air-conditioning type.
  3. [Water Tank Heating] = On, [Water Tank Heating Power] = 3000 W.
  4. Set DHW temperature + hysteresis; enable weekly sterilization 60–70 °C.
  5. [Number of modules] = 1.
There is no "DHW only" air-conditioning type. [Air Conditioning Type] offers exactly three options — [0] Heating Only · [1] Heating & Cooling (factory default) · [2] Cooling Only. A hot-water-only system comes from what you pipe and wire (no heating circuit) plus [Hot Water Function] = On, so leave this parameter at the default.

●● Level 2 · + Underfloor heating, controller = room thermostat

The wired controller on the wall is the room sensor (no separate stat). DHW priority; buffer feeds the floor. Backup heaters via Out B/C contactors.

🎛 Controller setup sequence

  1. [Air Conditioning Type] = [0] Heating Only (the manual also calls this "Single Heating") — or leave the [1] Heating & Cooling factory default if cooling may be added later.
  2. [Wire Controller Control] = Single Zone Temperature; mount controller indoors (home screen 2).
  3. [Room Temperature Control Scheme] = set so the controller uses room temperature.
  4. [Hot Water Function] = On; [Water Tank Heating] = On (3000 W).
  5. [Electric Heater 1 Power] = 3000 W (buffer backup).
  6. Leave thermostat terminals (1)–(2) linked; set a low underfloor flow-temp limit.

●●● Level 3 · + Buffer + 2 zones: radiators (A) + underfloor (B)

Two heating zones: radiators direct from the buffer (hot), underfloor via a mixing valve+pump (cool). DHW + both backup heaters as before.

🎛 Controller setup sequence

  1. [Air Conditioning Type] = [0] Heating Only, or keep the [1] Heating & Cooling factory default if the fan-coils will also cool.
  2. [Wire Controller Control] = Dual Zone (home screen 3 or 4).
  3. Zone A = water temp (≈45 °C); Zone B = water temp (≈32 °C) or room temp.
  4. Enable the Zone B mixing valve on Out 6 (+ Zone B pump on Out 7 if used).
  5. [Hot Water Function]=On, [Water Tank Heating]=On (3000 W); [Electric Heater 1 Power]=3000 W.
  6. Set the underfloor (Zone B) max flow-temp limit low.

●●●● Level 4 · + Backup heaters (cold climate / emergency)

Add the two 3 kW tank elements so the system never runs short on the coldest days or during fast DHW recovery. Full wiring diagrams are in §4.

➕ Advanced add-ons — each lands on one heat-pump terminal

You don't redesign anything — each extra feature just plugs into a specific terminal or relay. Here's exactly which one, the cable, and the setting.

☀ Solar water heating

🚰 Solar collector → the DHW tank's solar coil.

  • 🔌 Solar pump → relay Out D (230 V).
  • 🔌 Solar enable signal → inputs (4)(5) (dry contact).
  • 🔌 Collector sensor → the TSL sensor input (NTC, 2-wire).
  • 🎛 Enable the Solar function; solar takes priority over the heat pump for DHW when the sun is up.

🔆 PV self-consumption (Huawei only)

No power terminal — it's a communication link.

  • 🔌 EEBUS module on the 2nd RS485 bus (8)(9) = A2/B2.
  • Pairs with Huawei SmartAssistant / FusionSolar.
  • 🎛 [EEBUS Function] = Active, [OHPCF Configuration] = Config 4.
  • Uses surplus solar to over-heat the tank/buffer (free storage). Conflicts with Smart Grid — use one.

⚡ Smart Grid (SG / EVU)

Utility load-control (e.g. Germany §14a).

  • 🔌 SG → (12), EVU → (13) — dry-contact signals from the meter / ripple-control receiver.
  • The two on/off combinations give eco / normal / boost.
  • 🎛 [Smart Grid Function] = Use, [Configuration] = 6.

🌡️ Floor & extra temperature sensors

Sensors go to low-voltage inputs, never relays.

  • 🔌 An NTC sensor (floor / buffer / zone) → its sensor connector (2-wire, polarity-free).
  • 🔌 Floor over-temp safety stat → wired in series with the floor-zone pump/valve (cuts flow if the screed gets too hot).
  • The unit already reads floor-loop temp on TWout3 — usually no extra floor sensor is needed.
About the relays you keep hearing about: the board's 230 V relay outputs — A pump · B aux heater · C tank heater · D solar pump · 5/6/7 the 3-way/mixing valves — are how the heat pump switches external kit. Anything over 400 W (the 3 kW heaters, big pumps) must go through a contactor (§4). Sensors and dry-contact signals go to the low-voltage inputs (§3), never a relay output. 10 Controller — full operating manual

The controller cable is already done for you. A 10 m cable comes pre-wired into the heat pump — you just run the controller end indoors and clip the controller to the wall. Mount it inside, in a representative living space: it then doubles as the room thermostat for space heating (no separate stat needed). Keep it ~1.5 m up, away from drafts, direct sun and heat sources.

A · Daily operation

Turn a zone / DHW on-off

  1. On the main screen tap the area (heating zone or hot-water card).
  2. Tap [on/off] and confirm. The zone icon shows on/off.
  3. DHW won't turn on unless Hot Water Function = On (advanced).

  1. Tap [mode].
  2. Pick Heating, Cooling or Auto.

  1. Tap the temperature number in that area.
  2. Slide up/down to the value.
  3. Tap to save. It also updates in the app.

B · Pick your scenario (control scheme)

Your "scenario" = how many heating zones and whether each is controlled by water temperature or room temperature. It decides which of the four home screens you get. Set it once, in advanced parameters.

The four home screens

1 single zone · water temp + DHW
2 single zone · room temp + DHW
3 dual zone · water (A) + room (B) + DHW
4 dual zone · water (A) + water (B) + DHW

Screen 2 = the wired controller acts as the room thermostat (mount it indoors). Heating can run both zones; cooling runs Zone A only.

How to set it

  1. Menu → Advanced Para. → enter password 123456.
  2. Parameter Settings → System Parameters:
    • AC_TYPE [Air Conditioning Type] = [0] Heating Only · [1] Heating & Cooling (factory default) · [2] Cooling Only. Those are the only three options — there is no DHW-only setting.
    • WIRE_SW_TYPE (zone control) = single / double / three zone.
  3. Extended → Room Temperature Control Scheme = the scheme that makes the controller use room temperature (for screen 2/3).
  4. [Logout] to save and leave advanced mode.

D · Weather compensation (auto-lower the water temp)

What it does

Instead of a fixed flow temperature, the unit lowers the target water temperature as it gets warmer outside — big comfort + efficiency gain. Choose a curve per zone (A/B, heating & cooling separately): 8 low-temperature curves (underfloor) + 8 high-temperature curves (radiators) + one custom.

How to enable

  1. Menu → User Parameters → Zone A heating curve (and Zone B if used).
  2. Pick a low-temp curve for underfloor or a high-temp curve for radiators (or Disable for fixed temp).
  3. Steeper curve = warmer water in cold weather. Start mild and fine-tune over a season.

E · Hot water & Legionella sterilization

Hot water

  • Enable once: Advanced → Hot Water Function = On, Water Tank Heating = On, Water Tank Heating Power = 3000 W.
  • User sets the target in User Parameters → Hot Water Temp Set.
  • Force DHW Mode / Force Tank Heater in the menu for a one-off fast boost.
  • DHW has priority over heating (heating pauses briefly during a DHW cycle).

Sterilization (anti-Legionella)

  • Menu → Timer → Sterilization Timer: set day/time (typically weekly).
  • Temperature 60–70 °C, max cycle 90–300 min, high-temp hold 5–60 min.
  • Uses the DHW tank's 3 kW heater to reach the kill temperature.
  • Fit a blending/tempering valve at the taps if you store this hot (anti-scald).
